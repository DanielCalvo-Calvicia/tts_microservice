Prove that the deployed tts works inside the robot, with Brain as the only caller: Brain's preflight finds it within its 60 s wait, Brain uploads text and reads audio back, and the stream pair `set`/`get` works end to end.

### Steps 2 and 3 result (2026-11-09, run with the maintainer's yes and the real speaker)

**Setup.** A scratch native deployment with the tool (`oblivion.py --config <scratch>`, 7 min 8 s with the installs and the voice fetch), made from copies of the working trees of microphone, stt (local engine), tts, speaker and Brain; ai-agent is only listed in the layout (Brain's validation needs it) and was not started, and there was no stepper, so nothing could move. The services were stopped and the scratch folder removed afterwards.

**Step 2, the preflight.** Brain's log: `starting mandatory startup preflight`, `checking microservice field_service="tts"` answered 200, `all external microservices are active and ready attempts=1`, `startup preflight completed successfully`: **tts was ready on the first attempt** (within the 60 s wait). tts itself: `Piper voice loaded voice="en_GB-alan-medium" sample_rate=22050`, `TtsService initialized engine="PiperSpeechSynthesis"`, `GET /available` `is_available: true, reason: null` (no fallback).

**Step 3, Brain speaks.** Through Brain's `POST /tts/play?sample_rate=24000&channels=1`, three sentences, one after another, on the maintainer's real output device (`Speaker started device=3`, 24 kHz mono): `Hello. I am Oblivion. Systems online.` (37 characters), a 112-character sentence with numbers, and `Yes?`. Each answered `success: true`. tts logged `Shared stream generation completed` with 145,964 bytes (3.04 s), **343,068 bytes (7.15 s)** and 34,258 bytes (0.71 s), and Brain logged `TTS-to-speaker internal stream completed event` for each (the completed `TTS_OUTBOUND` read), so the acceptance criteria are met: tts ready in the preflight and a completed read for a sentence.

**Finding, in the speaker (R-6 confirmed on hardware):** the long sentence was **cut off**. The speaker logged `Finished consuming inbound audio stream queued_bytes=343068`, then 3.0 s later `WARNING Playback queue cleanup timed out; truncating` and `Playback session finalized ... written_bytes=145408`: **42 % of the audio (about 3.0 s of 7.15 s) was written to the device and the rest was dropped**, and the call still returned `success: true`. The first sentence (3.04 s) only just fitted (drained 2.7 s after the end of the stream). The cause is `_DRAIN_TIMEOUT_SECONDS = 3.0` in `speaker_microservice/infrastructure/outbound/sounddevice_playback/sounddevice_audio_playback.py`: tts synthesizes faster than real time, the whole reply is queued at once, and the speaker waits at most 3 s for it to play out. Any reply longer than about 3 s is truncated. The maintainer's own report of what was heard is not in this note.

**Not proven:** the sound itself was not judged in this run (the droid voice was judged on 2026-11-06, T-01.1.5), and a real ai-agent was not in the loop.

### Follow-up (2026-11-09): the speaker's truncation was fixed and re-checked

The speaker's fixed 3 s drain wait (R-6) was replaced by the duration of the queued audio plus 2 s (`speaker_microservice`, 8 new tests, 92 pass, 9 of 9 one-line mutations caught). Re-run on the same device with the same three sentences: Seen on a real device after the fix (2026-11-09, output device 3, 24 kHz mono, tts and the fixed speaker from scratch copies, each reply handed to the speaker in one request like Brain's stream): the 7.12 s sentence was written in full (`written_bytes` 341,578 of `queued_bytes` 341,578), the speaker answered after 7.27 s (it played for its whole duration), and the 3.05 s and 0.74 s sentences were also written in full; no truncation warning. The client was a small script, not Brain; the maintainer listened and confirmed that all three sentences were heard in full. Brain was not in this re-check.
